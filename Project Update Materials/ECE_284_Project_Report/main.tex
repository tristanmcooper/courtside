\begin{abstract}
Beach volleyball athletes experience large day-to-day performance variability that no single tool can explain: wearables report overnight recovery without same-day context, weather apps miss court-level microclimate, and mid-match self-report is impractical. We built and deployed \textbf{Courtside}, a working multi-modal sensing instrument that fuses (i) a custom courtside environmental node (ESP32-S3 with DHT11 air temperature/humidity, MLX90614 infrared sand-surface temperature, and an electret microphone as a wind/audio proxy), (ii) wearable recovery physiology (Apple Watch via automated HealthKit export; Oura Ring via API), (iii) coarse regional weather (Open-Meteo), and (iv) per-session subjective and objective performance logging, into a single per-session feature table feeding a four-stage factor-ranking analysis (correlation $\rightarrow$ sparse linear selection $\rightarrow$ tree ensembles $\rightarrow$ SHAP). The node streams over the athlete's phone hotspot to a cloud backend (FastAPI + PostgreSQL) and mobile web app that runs the live setup view, session recording, and evaluation entry. We validate the instrument end-to-end on a single-athlete pilot, including a 20-respondent player survey that shaped the feature set. We deliberately frame the contribution as a \emph{working personal-analytics instrument and pipeline}: with the small number of sessions collected to date, factor rankings are exploratory only, and we report that limitation honestly rather than overclaiming.
\end{abstract}

%===================================================================
\section{Motivation}
%===================================================================
Athletes know some days they play well and others they do not; isolating \emph{why} is hard because recovery, environment, and mindset interact and existing tools each measure one dimension in isolation. Commercial wearables (WHOOP, Oura, Garmin) produce readiness scores from overnight HRV and sleep but ignore same-day environmental load and subjective state. Weather apps report city-level conditions and miss court-level microclimate---wind is reshaped by surrounding terrain, and sand-surface temperature varies with grain size, moisture, and direct sun in ways ambient temperature cannot capture. Self-report is validated in sports psychology but collapses under in-match cognitive load.

\textbf{Gap.} No system fuses recovery, environment, subjective state, and performance for an individual beach volleyball athlete. \textbf{Who benefits.} The recreational or collegiate beach athlete who today has no way to contextualize a session; the broader health relevance is exertional-heat safety, which requires exactly this physiological--environmental--subjective fusion.

%===================================================================
\section{Related Work}
%===================================================================
\textbf{Wearable recovery.} WHOOP/Oura/Garmin daily readiness scores show reasonable resting HRV and sleep accuracy but are not contextualized against same-day environment or subjective state. \textbf{Environmental monitoring in sport.} FIVB enforces WBGT-based heat protocols and NATA publishes heat-illness positions, but these use coarse point measurements absent from recreational settings. \textbf{EMA.} Well-validated; voice/ASR lowers burden (scoped out here, see \S\ref{sec:scope}). \textbf{Wearables in beach volleyball.} Marzano-Felisatti et al.\ (2024) and the Sussex ``Wearables in Beach Volleyball'' project quantify movement/training-load from a single IMU modality; neither fuses recovery, environment, and subjective experience. \emph{Gap:} no published system performs this multi-modal joint analysis at the individual-athlete level.

\section{System Implemented}
%===================================================================
\begin{figure*}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\textwidth]{system_architecture.png}
    \caption{System architecture. The courtside node streams sensor readings over the athlete's phone hotspot to a cloud backend; the Apple Watch (and Oura) push recovery data to the same backend; the athlete logs subjective/objective outcomes from the mobile web app. All streams are time-aligned into one per-session feature table that feeds the four-stage factor-ranking analysis.}
    \label{fig:arch}

Courtside is fully built and deployed (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}); it is not a mockup. Components:

\textbf{Courtside node} (Fig.~\ref{fig:node}). An ESP32-S3 reads a \textbf{DHT11} (air temperature/humidity), an \textbf{MLX90614} non-contact IR thermometer (sand-surface temperature), and an analog electret microphone (peak-to-peak amplitude as a low-cost wind/audio proxy) at 1~Hz. Firmware connects to the athlete's iPhone Personal Hotspot and \texttt{POST}s each reading as JSON to the backend, with a serial CSV fallback. (The proposal specified a BME280; the delivered unit uses a DHT11---lower precision, no barometric pressure---a substitution discussed in \S\ref{sec:limits}.)

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.62\columnwidth]{node_beach.jpg}\hfill
    \includegraphics[width=0.36\columnwidth]{enclosure_cad.jpg}
    \caption{Left: the assembled node deployed courtside---ESP32-S3 + DHT11 + MLX90614 IR + electret mic in a 3D-printed enclosure, tripod-mounted on sand and powered from a USB battery for untethered operation. Right: the enclosure CAD (Fusion~360), showing the louvered vent for the air sensor, the open IR aperture aimed at the sand, and a 1/4-20 tripod mount.}
    \label{fig:node}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Cloud backend + app.} A FastAPI service with PostgreSQL, deployed on Render, ingests node and wearable data and serves a mobile web app. The app provides a \emph{live setup view} (real-time per-sensor plots plus a heuristic ``setup check,'' e.g.\ warning when the IR reading $\approx$ air temperature, indicating the sensor is not aimed at the sand), a \emph{recording} flow that bookmarks each session's start/stop so its sensor time-series is captured, and an \emph{evaluation} form (subjective 1--10 self-rating, optional peer/coach ratings, felt-wind, kills/errors, partner/opponent, notes, geolocation).

\textbf{Wearable ingestion.} Apple Watch data syncs automatically via the Health Auto Export app's REST automation (HRV [SDNN], resting HR, sleep, respiratory rate, and workouts), upserted by date. Oura is pulled via its v2 API as a \emph{second} recovery source, enabling cross-validation (Apple SDNN vs.\ Oura RMSSD). Regional weather (wind, gust, temperature, humidity) is pulled from Open-Meteo and kept clearly separate from on-court sensor readings as coarse context.

%===================================================================
\section{Data Handling and Feature Engineering}
%===================================================================
Sensor rows are stamped with server-receive time; each logged session windows the node readings to its \texttt{[start,end]} interval and aggregates them (mean/max sand and air temperature, mean humidity, mean/95th-percentile wind-audio). Daily wearable recovery is joined by date (Apple preferred, Oura fallback and cross-check). Apple Watch \emph{workouts} that overlap the session window yield in-session intensity (average/max HR, active energy); workouts in the prior 7 days yield a cross-training \emph{load} feature. Open-Meteo values are attached per session location. The unit of analysis is the session, appropriate given small $N$; the result is one tidy feature table per session.

%===================================================================
\section{Analysis Pipeline}
%===================================================================
The analysis is exploratory and descriptive. Four stages, all small-$N$ aware:
(1) Pearson and Spearman correlations of each feature against the subjective rating;
(2) Lasso regression with leave-one-out CV for sparse selection;
(3) random forest and gradient boosting feature importances;
(4) SHAP values from the gradient-boosting model.
We additionally compute \emph{leave-one-session-out} top-3 stability. The pipeline degrades gracefully: with too few sessions it emits correlations and an explicit caveat rather than fabricating a model. No $p$-values are reported as confirmatory and no model is released for deployment.

%===================================================================
\section{User-Need Validation: Player Survey (N=20)}
%===================================================================
Before further hardware investment we surveyed 20 players (UCSD men's/women's beach teams plus recreational, club, NCAA, and Open/AVP players) to validate need and prioritize features (Fig.~\ref{fig:survey}). \textbf{Wind ranked first} (4.05/5), validating the decision to prioritize wind sensing; recovery/tiredness, mental state, partner identity, and partner communication rounded out the top five; court noise, location, and pace of play ranked lowest and were dropped. Open responses repeatedly raised \emph{food timing}, \emph{soreness}, and \emph{warm-up quality}---now captured as lightweight subjective inputs. Across the cohort, 85\% expressed positive interest in the tool, with qualified interest centered on wearable integration and minimal in-session burden.

\section{Pilot Deployment and Validation}
%===================================================================
\textbf{Sensor validation (bench).} Each channel was validated for responsiveness (Fig.~\ref{fig:bench}): exhaling on the node produces the expected coupled spike in DHT11 temperature and humidity with exponential return to ambient; blowing across the microphone produces clear transient amplitude spikes above a stable baseline; and the MLX90614 tracks a warm surface in its field of view (an immediate $\sim$10\,$^\circ$C rise when aimed at skin). The Oura API pipeline was separately validated against a 21-day rolling window.

\begin{figure}[t]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\columnwidth]{esp32_bench.png}\hfill
    \includegraphics[width=0.49\columnwidth]{wind_proxy.png}
    \caption{Bench validation. Left: an operator exhalation near $t\approx14$~min produces a coupled temperature/humidity response. Right: the acoustic wind proxy responds to blown-air transients above a stable baseline.}
    \label{fig:bench}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Live courtside pilot.} The full wireless path was exercised on the beach: the node ran from a USB power bank, connected to the phone hotspot, and streamed to the cloud while the athlete viewed live readings on the dashboard to position the tripod. In the first instrumented day we recorded two sessions of $\sim$1{,}380 and $\sim$2{,}255 one-Hz sensor samples, with sand-surface temperatures of \textbf{40--43\,$^\circ$C}---substantially above the $\sim$26\,$^\circ$C air temperature, directly demonstrating the court-level microclimate that ambient/weather data cannot capture. Apple Watch recovery data for the same window arrived automatically via HealthKit export, and the build pipeline assembled a 42-column multi-modal feature table per session.

\textbf{Pipeline demonstration.} Fig.~\ref{fig:modeled} shows the pipeline's factor-ranking output. \emph{We stress this is a demonstration of the instrument's output, not a result:} with only a handful of sessions, correlations are unstable and not interpretable (a four-point correlation can exceed 0.8 by chance), which is exactly why the leave-one-session-out stability column and the small-$N$ caveat are built into the tool.

\section{Evaluation}
%===================================================================
Because the output is a per-session explanation, standard ML metrics do not apply; we evaluate the instrument on engineering criteria. \textbf{Data completeness:} for pilot sessions, the environmental, wearable, and subjective streams were captured end-to-end (the IR channel depends on the soldered sensor, now reliable). \textbf{Time alignment:} streams align to server-receive time and the session recording window; sub-second node cadence is confirmed by the sample counts above. \textbf{Sensor responsiveness:} validated per channel (above). \textbf{Ranking stability and face validity:} built into the pipeline (leave-one-session-out; comparison of top factors to the athlete's recollection) but \emph{not yet meaningful} at current $N$. A full evaluation requires $\geq$\,15--20 sessions; the instrument is now collecting them at low marginal effort.

%===================================================================
\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}
%===================================================================
\textbf{Small $N$ is the dominant limitation:} factor rankings are not statistically interpretable yet, and all output is framed descriptively. \textbf{Ground-truth quality:} the subjective 1--10 rating is the primary outcome; kills/errors are secondary and heavily \emph{confounded by partner and opponent} in 2v2 play, so they are reported as exploratory---we added optional \emph{peer and coach ratings} as higher-quality external ground truth, and split-by-own-contact statistics are future work. \textbf{Sensor fidelity:} the DHT11 is low-resolution ($\pm$2\,$^\circ$C, $\pm$5\%\,RH); the microphone is an uncalibrated qualitative wind proxy (validated against self-reported wind and regional weather, not an anemometer); regional weather is $\sim$1--3\,km grid-scale and deliberately coarse. \textbf{Scope cuts:} voice EMA and computer-vision kill/error extraction were de-scoped to keep the core instrument robust.
\label{sec:scope}

%===================================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
%===================================================================
We delivered a working, deployed multi-modal instrument and analysis pipeline for contextualizing beach volleyball performance, validated end-to-end on a single-athlete pilot and grounded in a 20-player needs survey. The methodological patterns---time-aligning heterogeneous low-cost streams, capturing court-level microclimate that regional data misses, small-$N$ feature ranking with honest stability reporting, and face-validity evaluation---generalize to other outdoor, individually-variable, heat-exposed settings. Immediate future work is straightforward: continue low-burden collection toward $\geq$\,15 sessions, add CV/peer-coach ground truth, and re-run the (already-built) ranking once $N$ supports it.
